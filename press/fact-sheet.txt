ONE-LINER
Hairspring measures how well a mechanical watch keeps time, using the
iPhone microphone — and tells you when it can't.

WHAT IT DOES
- Rate in seconds per day, with its measurement uncertainty computed
  from the actual scatter of the detected beats
- Beat error, measured from the escapement's own high-frequency signature
- Amplitude, reported as a band, because that is what the acoustic
  method honestly supports
- Timing graph, position runs, PDF reports, measurement history
- Measure the same watch twice and Hairspring states whether the two
  readings statistically agree — the check no other timegrapher app offers

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT
Hairspring refuses a rate it cannot defend. Too much scatter, an
implausible result, or timing too disturbed to number the beats produces
a stated refusal that names its cause and the fix — never a
plausible-looking wrong number. The analysis band its beat detector uses
was chosen by a pre-registered experiment: acceptance criteria written
down before the data was collected, with the first failed attempt
recorded as a failure.

NAME NOTE
Hairspring is unrelated to the discontinued watch-timing app of the same
name by another developer, which left the App Store years ago.
